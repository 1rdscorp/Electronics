      Connection order

      1. Measure every tap first: B(n) − B0 should read n × cell voltage, rising in even ~3.6–4.2 V steps. Fix any odd step before going on.
      2. Bolt the B− heavy lead from the pack to the BMS B− pad.
      3. Plug in the balance connector last on the BMS side (most BMS manuals ask for this order; follow yours).
      4. Connect P− to the output and charge negatives.
      5. With the switch OFF, connect B+ → Class T fuse → switch, then the output positive.
      6. Wake the BMS, check every cell reading, then connect the controller through the AS150 output plug.

      Cell colour

      X-ray makes cells and holders see-through so you can follow strips, the U-turn bus and current flow through the pack.

      140 cells hold about 2.5 kWh and can push thousands of amps into a short.

      Work on a non-conductive mat with insulated tools, no rings or watch. Cover every exposed strip and terminal you are not working on. Keep a fire blanket and a bucket of sand nearby.

      Build sequence

      1. Match cells. Same model and batch; resting voltage within 0.05 V across every cell before any parallel welding. Reject dented wraps.
      2. Load the holders. Each group is 2 rows × 5 cells. Flip orientation every group: G1 + up, G2 + down, and so on. Paper insulator ring on every + end.
      3. Fit the divider between column A and B before welding. Cells across it sit up to 13 series steps apart (≈ 55 V).
      4. Spot-weld the bottom strips (even nodes B0, B2 …) and the top strips (odd nodes B1, B3 …), plus the copper terminal tabs and the U-turn bus. Never solder onto a cell.
      5. Check every node with a meter against B0, then solder balance leads to strip tails, not to cells. Glue them down, Kapton over strip edges.
      6. Insulate and pad: fish paper top and bottom, closed-cell EVA foam to the walls, NTC 1 in the pack centre, NTC 2 at the U-turn.
      7. Wire the BMS in the order on the Wiring tab, then the fuse, switch, glands and AS150 output plug.
      8. Configure and test: first charge at low current, watch balance and temperatures, then a load test at the planned power.
      9. Install above bilge water, strapped down, ventilated compartment, fuse as close to the battery as the layout allows.

      BMS starting settings (Li-ion NMC)

      Starting points only. Your cell datasheet and BMS manual win where they differ.

      Marine notes

      1. Use tinned marine cable and crimped, heat-shrunk lugs; corrosion is the usual failure on boats.
      2. Every fuse and switch in the 58.8 V circuit must carry a DC voltage rating of at least 60 V. Many automotive fuses are only 32 V or 58 V DC rated.
      3. Marine lithium practice (ABYC E-13 in the US) points to a Class T main fuse for its high interrupt rating.
      4. Seal every penetration with a gland and fit a pressure-equalising vent so the box breathes without pulling in water.
